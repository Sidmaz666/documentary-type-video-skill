On the eighth of November, twenty twenty-six, the founder and chief executive of NVIDIA sent a letter to his employees. Two sentences in, he wrote: I was unfaithful to my wife. He gave no name. But the board had already seen what American intelligence agencies had concluded — and within hours, it announced an independent investigation. Not into an affair. Into a foreign intelligence effort, reportedly aimed at the most powerful man in artificial intelligence.

Jensen Huang co-founded NVIDIA in 1993 and has run it ever since. In July 2025, NVIDIA became the first company ever to cross four trillion dollars. Three months later, the first to cross five. Every frontier AI model on Earth runs, in large part, on NVIDIA silicon — which makes who may buy it one of the sharpest edges of American power. And Huang was no bystander to that debate. He urged Washington to ease the export ban. He got the President's ear.

The woman at the center of this is another Huang — Alice Huang, no relation. A Chinese citizen. Reported as a top executive at Megaspeed International — a Singapore company renting computing power, built on NVIDIA chips, to Chinese firms. Not charged. Not heard from. And Jensen Huang has never said her name. What the press calls an affair, and what intelligence agencies call an asset — they overlap around this one person.

According to reporting based on US intelligence assessments, surveillance unmasked a contact that identified Jensen Huang as the American on the other end of the line — and concluded Alice Huang was an asset of China's Ministry of State Security, recruited to cultivate him, to potentially influence the chip flow. A memo circulated to top officials in the spring of twenty twenty-six. The Financial Times reported it described a romantic relationship. Secretary of State Rubio oversaw it. President Trump was briefed about a year before it went public — and chose not to act.

Before you call it settled — hold on. Neither the CIA nor the White House has confirmed it. NBC said it had not independently confirmed it. The Wall Street Journal reported she vanished into China in late 2024 — and Singapore's Ministry of Home Affairs replied flatly: no such investigations existed. China called it groundless speculation. An allegation this serious, and a denial this absolute — both sit on the table at once. Which is why the board's only public words were cautious: no reason to believe national security or NVIDIA secrets were compromised.

Megaspeed — once 7Road, a Chinese-backed firm — renamed in 2023. Alice Huang was its managing director for eight months. Its Malaysian affiliate, Speedmatrix, reportedly bought at least four-point-eight billion dollars of equipment containing NVIDIA technology between June 2024 and February 2026. The Commerce Department probed it — then the case moved to Justice. Huang told Bloomberg his dealings were normal and not unusual. NVIDIA says the diversion allegations were thoroughly investigated and debunked last year.

None of this is new. Margaretha Zelle — Mata Hari — was executed by France in 1917. A century later, declassified files showed British intelligence found nothing she passed on. East Germany's Stasi ran an entire directorate of Romeos — staged-romance recruiters, harvesting the loyalties of West German officials. Monica Witt, a US Air Force intelligence specialist, fell into an online romance with Iranian-linked operatives — and defected in 2013. And from 2011 to 2015, an operative known as Christine Fang cultivated American politicians — the FBI captured romantic relationships with at least two mayors. The playbook is older than the microchip. Access. Intimacy. Leverage. But — and this is the discipline — the fact that agencies use romance does not prove this case was an operation. Mata Hari is the warning.

Why does Washington care? Because of what NVIDIA sells. Since October 2022, America has required licenses for its most advanced AI chips to reach China. In 2025, the China-specific H20 lost that license too. In December, the President approved H200 sales — a twenty-five percent cut to the US — and by May 2026, roughly ten firms were cleared, and zero had taken delivery. Beijing simply said no.

So when an alleged asset of Chinese intelligence reportedly cultivated the man who shapes America's AI-hardware policy — who dines with the President — every call and every favor becomes consequential. Republicans in the House demanded a closed-door briefing. On November ninth, NVIDIA fell about four-point-three percent — one hundred ninety billion dollars, in a day. A shareholder suit followed within hours — for failing to disclose an affair. Not for proven espionage.

So. What do we actually know? Confirmed: Jensen Huang admitted being unfaithful. The board opened an independent investigation. China calls it all smears — on the record, twice. Reported: intelligence assessed Alice Huang as a Ministry of State Security asset. Unknown: what, if anything, she passed. And whether this was an operation, an accident, or coincidence. The evidence does not let anyone say honey trap as a fact. It lets everyone ask the question.

The oldest trick in history. Two people. One runs the machine that runs the modern world. The other, reportedly, worked for its oldest adversary. Whether this was an operation, an accident, or human weakness wearing a badge — nobody credible can say yet. But one thing is certain: the most guarded secrets on Earth now move as much through trust as through technology. And trust, it turns out, is the one system nobody has patched.
